How it works

Estimate what was lost. Then move what was left.

Every quantity comes from FreshRetailNet-50K except two scenario inputs: how much stock is left unsold at stores that never ran out, and when a transfer can reach the shelf.

Learn each store-product's day

Average sales for every daytime hour, using only hours when the product was in stock. Stockout hours never pull the curve down.

Estimate lost demand

On a stockout day, apply the curve to the empty hours, scaled to that day's level from the hours that were stocked. Tested on a held-out week by hiding hours we know.

Plan the transfers

Within one city, product and day, a linear program sends unsold stock to stores that run out after the transfer arrives. It fills higher-price stores first and skips a move when handling costs more than the sale.

Is the lost-demand estimate any good?

Fully stocked days in the held-out week (26 Jun – 2 Jul 2024). We hid every hour from the cut-off onward and predicted those sales from curves learned on the earlier 90 days. Bias is the error in total hidden sales.

Data and limitations

FreshRetailNet-50K
Dingdong fresh retail: 50,000 store-product series with hourly sales and hourly stockout flags. 90 training days and a 7-day evaluation week. Sales are normalised.
Not in the data
Stock on hand, store locations and transfer costs. Unsold stock and arrival time are scenarios. Every pair of stores in a city is treated as reachable at the same cost.
Hindsight
The replay knows which stores will run out. A live system would forecast it, so these figures are an upper bound for each scenario.
