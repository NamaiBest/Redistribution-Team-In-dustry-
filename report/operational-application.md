# Operational application and managerial implications

Perishable shortages and excess stock can coexist in different regions, leaving buyers unserved while produce loses shelf life elsewhere. India faced a comparable distribution problem in July 2023, when the Department of Consumer Affairs directed cooperatives to procure tomatoes from mandis in Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, and Maharashtra for distribution in markets facing rising retail prices. Our project turns this type of decision into a browser tool for a wholesaler, farmer producer organisation, or procurement coordinator. The market view combines reported mandi arrivals with wholesale prices. A low-arrival, high-price signal identifies a market worth checking. A coordinator can then enter saleable stock, confirmed buyer orders, remaining shelf life, and freight quotes to compare feasible transfers.

The model fills local orders first and chooses direct shipments subject to receiving requirements, travel time, lane capacity, and in-transit loss. In the August 2025 tomato replay, it delivered a modeled 17.80 tonnes across four lanes. Simulated spoilage fell from 11.86 to 3.43 tonnes compared with keeping stock local under the same assumptions. Reducing remaining shelf life from 24 to 12 hours removed all proposed tomato lanes. These figures illustrate how the decision changes with operating conditions. A field pilot could test the workflow on one corridor by recording outgoing and received edible weights, rejected stock, delivery times, and fulfilment against matched days without transfers.
